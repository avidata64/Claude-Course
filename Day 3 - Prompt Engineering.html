Prompt Engineering for Banking

How to ask AI clearly: the core techniques, simple banking examples and the mistakes to avoid

Before we start

How a chat tool works

1. You type

You write a message. This is your prompt.

2. It is sent

The tool sends the whole conversation so far to the model.

3. It writes

The model writes the reply one small piece (token) at a time.

4. You reply

Your next message is added, and the whole conversation is sent again.

So the model only knows what is in this conversation. That is why a clear message matters.

Prompt Engineering for Banking

What and why

Instructions in everyday words

Normal software

A programmer writes exact rules in code. If a rule is missing, the software does nothing.

An AI chat tool

You write instructions in plain words. If you leave a gap, the model fills it with a guess.

Prompt engineering is getting good at writing those instructions, so the guesses are the ones you want.

Useful for relationship managers, operations, compliance and anyone who writes at work.

The hook · Try 2

The same task, asked clearly

Better prompt

You are a retail bank marketing writer. Write a 2-sentence SMS for our new savings account (interest rate [X]%, no minimum balance). Audience: young working adults. Tone: friendly, not pushy. End with one step to open it online. No emojis.

Response (illustrative)

Short, on-brand and ready to send once a person checks the facts.

Prompt Engineering for Banking

Foundation

How the model ‘thinks’

It predicts the next token

A token is a word or part of a word. It picks the next one from everything so far.

It has no memory of old chats

Unless the information is in the current conversation, it does not know it.

Your words steer it

Context, detail and structure in your prompt shape what comes next.

So: give relevant context

More relevant context gives more relevant and more consistent answers.

Prompt Engineering for Banking

Commanding versus steering · 1

Commanding: just tell it what to do

Commanding prompt

Write an email to a customer who was charged twice.

Response (illustrative)

Dear Customer, We are very sorry about the duplicate charge on your account. We understand how frustrating this is. Please be assured we are looking into it and will get back to you shortly. If you have any questions, do not hesitate to contact us. Kind regards, [Your Name]

The model chose the length, tone and content. No timeline, and it is long and generic.

Prompt Engineering for Banking

Commanding versus steering · 2

Steering: you choose the direction

Steering prompt

You are a customer care officer at a retail bank. Write a short, apologetic email to a customer who was charged twice for one card payment. Say we will refund within [X] working days. Tone: professional and reassuring. Under 80 words.

Response (illustrative)

Subject: Your duplicate card charge. Dear [Name], I am sorry you were charged twice for one payment. We have started an investigation, and any duplicate amount will be refunded within [X] working days. Please reply if you have questions. Kind regards, Customer Care Team

Short, specific and reassuring, with a clear timeline.

Technique 1 · Be specific

A weak prompt

Weak prompt

Write a reminder about a loan payment.

Response (illustrative)

Subject: Loan Payment Reminder. Dear Customer, This is a reminder that your loan payment is due soon. Please ensure that your payment is made on time to avoid any late fees or penalties. Thank you for your attention to this matter.

Too long for a text message, stiff, and a little threatening. We never said where it will be sent.

Prompt Engineering for Banking

Technique 1 · Be specific

Add role, audience, tone and format

Strong prompt

Role: You are a customer care officer at a retail bank.
Audience: customers with a personal loan.
Tone: polite and clear, not threatening.
Task and format: write one text message under 160 characters reminding them their payment of [amount] is due on [date].

Response (illustrative)

Hi [Name], a friendly reminder: your loan instalment of [amount] is due on [date]. Please make sure funds are available. Need help? Call us anytime.

Short, friendly and ready to send.

Technique 2 · Examples

No examples (zero-shot)

Prompt with no examples

Is this customer message a complaint, a question or praise?
“My card was declined and nobody called me back.”

Response (illustrative)

This message appears to be a complaint. The customer is unhappy that their card was declined and that no one followed up with them. It would be a good idea for the bank to contact them quickly and apologise.

Right answer, but wordy. If you have 500 messages to sort, you want one word each.

Prompt Engineering for Banking

Technique 2 · Examples

Show two to four examples (few-shot)

Prompt with examples

Label each customer message as COMPLAINT, QUESTION or PRAISE.
“Why was I charged a fee?” → QUESTION
“The app keeps crashing on me.” → COMPLAINT
“Thanks, the staff were very helpful!” → PRAISE
“My card was declined and nobody called me back.” →

Response (illustrative)

COMPLAINT

One word, in the format you showed. One example is called one-shot; two to four is few-shot.

Technique 3 · Step by step

A quick answer to a tricky question

Prompt

A bat and a ball cost $1.10 in total. The bat costs $1.00 more than the ball. How much does the ball cost?

Response (a hurried answer can look like this)

The ball costs 10 cents.

Check: if the ball is $0.10, the bat is $1.10 and the total is $1.20, not $1.10. The quick answer is wrong.

Prompt Engineering for Banking

Technique 3 · Step by step

Ask it to show its working

Better prompt

A bat and a ball cost $1.10 in total. The bat costs $1.00 more than the ball. How much does the ball cost? Think step by step and show your working before the final answer.

Response (illustrative)

Let the ball cost x. Then the bat costs x + $1.00. Together: x + (x + $1.00) = $1.10, so 2x = $0.10 and x = $0.05. The ball costs $0.05 (5 cents) and the bat costs $1.05.

You can check each step. Phrases to use: “Think step by step.” “Show your reasoning.”

Technique 4 · Ask for a format

No format asked: you get paragraphs

Prompt (made-up details)

Compare these savings accounts. A: 1.5% interest, no minimum balance, no fee. B: 2.0% interest, S$1,000 minimum, S$2 monthly fee. C: 2.5% interest, S$5,000 minimum, S$5 monthly fee.

Response (illustrative)

Account A offers 1.5% interest with no minimum balance or fee, making it the simplest option. Account B offers a higher 2.0% but needs S$1,000 and charges S$2 a month. Account C pays the most at 2.5% but needs S$5,000 and charges S$5 a month.

Correct, but hard to scan or paste into a document.

Technique 4 · Ask for a format

Ask for a table

Better prompt

Compare Accounts A, B and C using only these details. Reply as a table with columns: Account, Interest, Minimum balance, Monthly fee. If a value is missing, write [CHECK]. A: 1.5%, no minimum, no fee. B: 2.0%, S$1,000 minimum, S$2 fee. C: 2.5%, S$5,000 minimum, S$5 fee.

Technique 5 · Constraints

Say what you do not want

Length

“Under 100 words.” “Exactly 3 bullet points.”

Tone

“No slang.” “Do not over-apologise.”

Format

“One short paragraph, no bullets.”

Scope

“Do not promise a refund.” “Do not give reasons.”

Limits like these are sometimes called guardrails. Next: a customer asks why a payment is on hold.

Prompt Engineering for Banking

Technique 5 · Constraints

Without limits

Prompt

Reply to this customer: “Why is my transfer on hold?”

Response (illustrative)

I am sorry your transfer is on hold. This usually happens when our security checks flag a payment for review, for example when the amount is large or unusual. These checks normally clear within 1 to 2 working days, and your money will then be released.

It guessed a reason and promised a timeline. A bank may not be able to say either.

Prompt Engineering for Banking

Technique 5 · Constraints

With limits

Better prompt

You are a customer care officer. Reply to this customer: “Why is my transfer on hold?” Write two polite, neutral sentences. Say the payment is under routine review and we will contact them. Do not give reasons. Do not promise a release time. Offer a human agent.

Response (illustrative)

Thank you for contacting us. Your payment is under routine review, and we will be in touch as soon as we can. If you would like to speak to someone now, I can connect you with a member of our team.

No guessed reasons, no promised time, and a human is offered.

Treat it as a conversation

You

Technique 7 · Interview style

Let the model interview you

Instead of guessing what context to give, state the goal and ask the model to ask you questions first.

How to do it

  1. Give the task in a sentence or two.
  2. Ask it to interview you, one question at a time.
  3. Answer each question briefly.
  4. It writes once it has enough.

Why and when

We forget audience, tone and limits because we do not know the model needs them. Its questions show the gaps.

Use it for important or complex tasks. For a small task, one clear prompt is faster.

Prompt Engineering for Banking

Technique 7 · Example

The model asks, you answer

I need a short welcome email for new savings account customers. Before you write it, interview me: ask any questions you need, one at a time. When you have enough, say “I have enough” and write the email.

Model: Who is the email for?

You: Young adults opening their first savings account.

Model: What tone would you like?

You: Friendly and simple, with no jargon.

Model: Is there anything it must include?

You: No minimum balance, open online, and [X]% interest.

Technique 7 · Result

Then it writes, with full context

Model: I have enough. Here is the email.

Subject: Welcome to your new savings account

Hi [Name], welcome! Your account has no minimum balance and earns [X]% interest, so you can start saving with any amount. Log in online to set up your first deposit in minutes. We are glad you chose us.

Warm regards, [Bank name]

Audience, tone and key points are all right the first time, with no “wrong draft, then fix” rounds.

Technique 8 · Let it decide

“Do whatever you think is best”

You can leave the choices to the model: the angle, the style, even the format.

Good for

Ideas, names, first drafts, and times when you do not yet know what you want.

Watch out

Results vary and may not fit your need. It is hard to judge them against a target you never set.

A good pattern

Let it decide first. Pick what you like. Then steer it with specific instructions.

Prompt Engineering for Banking

Technique 8 · Example

When you leave the choice to the model

Open-ended prompt

Write something to encourage our customers to start saving. Do whatever you think is best.

Response (illustrative)

Small steps, big savings. Put aside just S$5 a day and in a year you will have S$1,825. Start today, one coffee at a time.

The model chose the angle, the style and even the figures. Nice for ideas; you would still check and rewrite before using it.

Banking use case · KYC

Reading a company document

KYC means “know your customer”: the bank checks who a client is. Analysts often retype details from company documents.

Simple prompt (made-up document)

Extract the details from this company document: Sunrise Example Pte Ltd. Address: 10 Example Road, Singapore. Directors: Tan Wei Ming, Lim Hui Ling. Shareholders: Tan Wei Ming (60%), Lim Hui Ling.

Response (illustrative)

Company: Sunrise Example Pte Ltd. Address: 10 Example Road, Singapore. Directors: Tan Wei Ming, Lim Hui Ling. Shareholders: Tan Wei Ming 60%, Lim Hui Ling 40%.

The document never gave Lim Hui Ling’s share. The model filled the gap with a guess.
